H.264 luma round-trip check

Encodes a known 0..255 luma ramp, plays it, and samples it back four ways. A data-encoded dataset is only viable where the sampled value equals the source value.

render is the row that matters. The globe draws through a WebGL texture, and since the probe moved to glLumaSampler the hover readout does too, so this column models both.

The three readout* columns sample through a 2D canvas (drawImage + getImageData, plain / colorSpace: 'srgb' / one full-size blit). That is what the probe used to do. They are kept as diagnostics: Safari on macOS and iOS transforms every one of them identically — endpoints preserved, up to 11 codes of error in between — which is why the probe no longer reads that way. Expect them to fail on Safari; that is the finding, not a regression.

H_ceiling_8k is the Phase 0 probe (docs/DATA_ENCODED_RESOLUTION_PLAN.md). Same encoder settings as G, at 8192×4096. Its capability row — decodes, readyState, decoded size, MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE, texImage2D, and whether a known texel survives — is the record that plan gates on. A failure there is a result to write down for this device, not a bug.

The native column is the one that cannot be faked. A device that decodes the frame but quietly halves it would keep every ramp band intact and report good values that were never measured. The frame carries isolated single-texel spikes for that reason: they read ~252 at full resolution and collapse to ~63 through a 2× downscale.
